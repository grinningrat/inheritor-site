# How to Use This Book
The Mistlands consist of keyed locations of the following types:
- **Settlements**, or areas of relative civilization.
- **Wilderness**, or places of natural make.
- **Landmarks**, or places of specific import.
- **Dungeons**, or the realms of monsters.
A forthcoming map will display the locations of each, along with the page number they are found in this book.

# Ashwharf
Clusters of stilt-lifted huts house **Ashwharf’s** 300 denizens off the salt-lashed coast of the [[Misted Sea]]. The people speak [[Proficiencies Old#Trade|Trade]] but are largely illiterate—only one in twenty can read. They are a fishing and diving folk, spending much of their lives in and around the water, though in recent days they keep to shore for fear of the overhead sound of wings like thunder.

**Klaos (Human, 2 HD, War Sickle, *[[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Descriptions - Magick Items#Closed-Eye Cloak|Closed-Eye Cloak]]* over Mail, Claim: Ashwharf)** has led them for nearly four decades. He despises magick in all forms—a hatred born from his sister Ula’s disappearance over sixty years ago. Many in town share this contempt, directing it chiefly at the [[Inheritor/Setting Guide/The Mistlands/Bell Watchers|Bell Watchers]] to the south. Despite calls to clear the place out, Klaos stays his hand: a slumbering **Molten Drake** resides in the cliffside caves there. The aged leader knows to send a militia is to send **Talum**, a needless death in his eyes. 

Outsiders, however, would earn a hefty reward for the drake’s head: 125 sb and the Closed-Eye Cloak off of Klaos’s own back.

**Captain Talum (Human, 2 HD, War Sickle, Jack, Claim: Ashwharf Militia)**, is the closest thing Klaos has to a son and serves his lord in all expected capacities. He also quietly seeks the old man’s position, believing Klaos to be too old, too fearful of the world’s evils. 
**Sayara**, a jewelry purveyor in town, feeds this flame in him in their quiet moments together. 

She was instructed to do so by a **Sea Witch (Undead, 3 HD†, *[[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Descriptions - Magick Items#Sailor’s Folly|Sailor’s Folly]]*, Unarmored)** who lairs in the underwater caves just offshore. The witch gifts Sayara scoured riches of the deep she then sells in town; riches the witch curses to subtly manipulate events in town.

**80 footmen, 2 skiffs, 15 fishing boats, and 10 canoes**

# Greenbury
Hollow chimes echo before the village is seen. A temple, half-rendered incorporeal, shadows an abandoned courtyard. Squat hovels radiate outwards from the temple, each built for smaller frames, doorways encrusted with mucus and salt. The windowless temple's walls are carved with spiraling grooves meant for webbed fingers to trace in prayer.

80 spirits orbit the temple exterior, gathering around visitors, gesturing to a passage beneath the holy place. Unable to speak, they cannot explain why they encircle without entering—only that something terrible resides below. Past the hovels, hidden from the courtyard, rows of crucified creatures of all kin hang submerged to their necks, their bodies stripped to bone.

They owe this degradation to **Y'rtul the Bloodletter (Beastman, 4HD, *Bloodletter's Pike*, Cuirass)**, a vicious merman who hunts the local waterways. There is a 20% chance he is already here, otherwise he returns on a 2-in-6 per turn.

Y'rtul serves **Aracioth (Undead Dragon, 7HD†[−], Curse Breath 3d6, Claws as War Sickles, Scales as Lamellar, Claim: Greenbury)**, a wingless drake laired in the temple's flooded underbelly, his scales warped and corroded by black iron.

Aracioth rarely leaves, relying on Y'rtul to bring souls to sustain him. Y'rtul believes these sacrifices will grant him dominion over the waterways by way of favor with the dragon. Aracioth despises needing the merman and plans to kill him once strong enough.

Aracioth's hoard contains:
- Loose copper bits and silver bobs, totaling 300 cb in value
- ***Spectral Chainmail***
- A **Weeping Ironsore (Plant, 5HD[+], Cannot Attack, Skin as Mail)** expelling 1d6 phials of ***iron grease*** each day until killed

# Water Wheel
The groaning of a dilapidated water wheel rumbles across the valley, an adjoining structure sitting squat besides. The stream that ran through it has become little more than an pitious rivulet, causing the wheel's paddles to start and stop frequently.

Within the structure, half-mad cackles and the sounds of metal tinkering can be heard. Gray-skinned **Silverfisher Argid (Human, 1HD, Hammer as Club, Unarmored, Claim: Water Wheel)** toils here in all manner of artificery. He welcomes any visitors through his workshop of unfinished projects. Gears, silver wire, and clockwork parts lie strewn about in haphazard piles, like a metallic charnel house.

A large, empty frame sits in the center of the room, the only cleared spot of the entire building; Argid claims his "magnum opus"—a **Filigree Knight (Construct, 4HD, 3 x Sword, Plating as Lamellar)**,fully restored from the days of the War of Iron and Silver—up and left in the middle of the night without a sound. He makes it aware that any who bring him clues as to its whereabouts will be well-rewarded for their efforts (200 gl).

Outside, a colony of 2d20 **Silver Eaters (Beast, ½ HD, Acid 1d4, Unarmored)** have made their home in the crevices of the water wheel feasting on the scattered remnants of Argid's tinkering. These miniscule, slug-like creatures devour alchemical metals (silver, gold, others) and expelling a phial's worth of ***metallurgic secretion*** each week.

Argid specializes in crafting and trading silver equipment and alchemical metals.

# Lunar Chapel
An ancient stone monument hides shrouded within the dense wood, older than the trees that surround it; a crumbling relic, its pillars and steps lined with fading witch-hexes. At its heart stands an ash-white statue of a woman—head missing, right hand raised, left hand low; both palms stained black.

Shadows haunt here; a group of seven **Dark Sisters (Human, 2HD, Grimoire [2, Lunar], *[[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Magick Items#Runeward Dagger|Runeward Dagger]]*, Unarmored)** of varied ages and wiry frames that belie their unnatural strength. The Sisters revere the **Mother Moon** as a divine, lupine woman, whom they believe departed the heavens to hunt her cosmic enemies.

Once devout **Sisters of the Moon**, they turned to sacrilege, supping the blood of one another and that of captured victims in fell rituals. These rites have corrupted them, giving them a feral hunger for flesh and a dark mastery of hexes. They await the moon's return to the skies above—from her maw a torrent of blood is to flood the world, drowning those found unworthy. 

Any approaching woman may have a short window to speak with the Sisters before they turn on her. Any approaching man is attacked long before he sees them.

A short distance from the chapel lies a shallow mass grave, within which lies hidden cache of mismatched items, stolen or salvaged during the sisters' hunts:

- Faded love letters, still faintly smelling of ***[[Inheritor/Inheritor's Guide/Ingredients#Elia Petals|elia petals]]***
- 2 x phials of ***[[Inheritor/Inheritor's Guide/Ingredients#Iron-Shaled Blood|iron-shaled blood]]***
- Shredded ***Grimoire [3, Dread Rites]***
- A dusty vial of ***asterial concoction***, stamped with a crescent moon

# Bell Watchers
Three rusted, gargantuan monuments to the [[Black Iron Faith]] loom off the coastline’s cliffs. Each served as a tower that once held devout soldiers; only one of them—[[#the Third Tower]]—could hope to serve this functionality today. 

Now empty of any such defenders—or walkways to get there—the only entrance is through a gaping hole in the monument’s head. The air is heavy with the brine of the sea, the blood-like tang of the iron-cursed, and the acrid stench of burnt metal.

On windy days, the Watchers emit a haunting song through their pockmarked surfaces. This has attracted nearby **Ironmad (Undead, 1HD[-], Unarmed, Skin as Mail)**, who number nearly 80 at the cliff’s edge. More often than not, one teeters off the edge and tumbles the nearly a hundred feet into the briny depths below where they drown but do not die. 

They ignore a jagged ledge just below the cliff’s edge, winding downward along its face. The ledge terminates at an open cavern, a mere 30 feet above the crashing waves. **Qalcirath (Dragon, 6HD†, Fire Breath 3d6, Claws as War Sickles, Scales as Coat of Plates)** hoards his ill-gotten wealth at the cave; 50% chance he slumbers upon it now. If not here, the drake stalks the coastline from here all the way up to [[Old Silvervein Mine]]—returning on 1-in-6 every [[Turns|Turn]]. He can detect disturbances to his hoard from a great distance away, racing back on furious wings in 1 exploration turn if his ire is raised.

Inside the cave, strewn about the floor from wall to wall:

- A chest of 10,000 cb worth of [[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Silver Bob|silver bobs]] and [[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Gold Piece|gold pieces]], along with 5,000 cb worth of [[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Glint|glints]] and [[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Shimmer|shimmers]].
- Three scorched, but fully-intact [[Equipment#Weapons|Coats of Plates]]
- 6 large casks of Drah’nakhian Blackwine in a charred wagon missing its wheels, each worth 1000 cb.
- ***[[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Embered Carapace|Embered Carapace]]***
## The Third Tower
The Third Tower holds five levels, the two bottommost of which are submerged in frigid waters.
1. **Head of the Third Tower**. Open to the prickling, briny air. 1d4 **Shardstung Harpies (Beastfolk, 1HD, Claws as Daggers, Unarmored)** argue here, working on a plan to avoid tithing to Qalcirath. **Erish Silverteeth (Beastfolk, 2HD, ***[[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Remorse|Remorse]]***, Unarmored)** stews nearby, plucking feathers from her plumage. A large hole drops 20 feet into the Upper Torso of the Third Tower.
2. **Upper Torso of the Third Tower**. The floor has been eaten away by time, leaving only angry, rust-red jets of iron in twisting formations. Swollen wooden planks connect the remnants of the floor, one of the floor-platforms holding a heap of frozen clothes near the remains of a pathetic campfire. Nearly 50 feet down, in complete darkness, sloshing can be heard.
	- Cracking the clothes apart, 20±1d6 gl and an ornate, bone-white pocket knife can be found
3. **Lower Torso of the Third Tower**. Pitch-dark, waist-deep freezing water. 2 naked **Ironmad Reavers (Undead, 2HD[-], Mace, Skin as Mail)** and an **Ironmad Berserker (Undead, 3HD, *[[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Nail|Nail]]*, Skin as Mail)**, amble aimlessly, mumbling in broken Blacktongue. They cannot be reasoned with, though the water slows them significantly. A submerged ladder leading downwards towards the Upper Base of the Third Tower sits in the center of the room, but the Ironmad have piled debris to cover it—taking two exploration turns to fully excavate it.
4. **Upper Base of the Third Tower**. Pitch-dark, completely submerged. Must test PHY every exploration turn or lose 1HP / turn until out of the water. A large hole in the side of the tower allows the sea to enter and exit unimpeded. Three weeks ago, a large sea creature made the lower tower its hunting grounds, but was badly wounded from the tower’s jagged iron. As such, it has become blinded with the curse of black iron. The **Ironmad Squid Serpent (Undead Beast, 5HD[-], Crush 2d6 requiring PHY test to escape, Skin as Mail)** can only sense by blood. In its belly is the legendary ***[[Inheritor/Warden's Guide/Treasure#Dart of Old Thunder|Dart of Old Thunder]]***.
5. **Lower Base of the Third Tower**. Glinting light can be seen barely shining at the bottom of the tower’s base. A warped, oxidized dias rests, with a blackened corpse overtaken by iron-shaled mussels rigidly standing at it. In its hands, a sphere of perfect, rustless black iron: an Iron Core, a sentient vestige of the Ebon Lord’s will.
