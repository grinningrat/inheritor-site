# Coinage
[[The Mistlands]] are a place of vastly different peoples, and with vastly different peoples comes vastly different currency. Most locations will accept any modern coinage, though some may restrict or enforce particular coins.
## Modern Coins
> [!info] Modern Coinage
> Modern coins consist of **copper bits (cb)**, **silver bobs (sb)**, **gold pieces (gp)**, **glints (gl)**, and **shimmers (sh)**, with copper bits being the standard.
### Copper Bit (cb)
The Old Man, a bite, the humble copper bit. Standard of the Mistlands and its isolated denizens. Where some deal in gold, others ban glints and shimmers, and more refuse uncommon coinage; all take copper.
- Found virtually anywhere trade occurs
- Minted by an empire long since passed; only standard for relative lack of value to near everyone
### Silver Bob (sb)
*Worth 10 cb*
The Shrike, a tenner, the workhorse silver bob. Less common than copper but far more prevalent than gold. Most who take copper take silver as well, with silver lending more freely due to its better returns.
- Found virtually anywhere larger trades occur
- Originally minted by the [[Lords]] in the [[Timeline#Age of Argentum|Age of Argentum]], as an attempt at national currency.
### Gold Piece (gp)
*Worth 100 cb*
The Sun, a setter, the noble gold piece. Trades between hands of high nobility, merchant-lords, and long-lived contracts. Flashing a setter is likely to bring bad company, thus few merchants deal in them.
- Found behind closed-doors, among the aristocracy, and dangerous criminals
- Originally minted by the insectoid dwarves of the [[Cinder King]], in their subterranean gemstone halls
### Glint (gl)
*Worth 25 cb*
Made from a hard, opalescent mineral called [[ryal]] that naturally comes apart into small, marble-like beads when extracted. Being immune to magick in all forms, glints are the trading currency of choice for peddlers of arcane wares.
### Shimmer (sh)
*Worth 250 cb*
Plates of ryal form beautiful textures worth a small fortune. Exceedingly rare to find for ryal's propensity to break down into glints, shimmers trade among noble merchants and high arcanists more as a status symbol than for its efficacy.
## Special Coins
> [!info] Special Coinage
> Special coins are of more value to a collector than a merchant, and as such often aren't often accepted as legal tender. In the Mistlands, these are **flatshells**, **green leptons**, **bronze gudgeons**, **platinum drachs**, **spirit seeds**, **shieldmate denars**, **electrum staters**, and **spun sylmats**.
### Flatshell
Fished near 300 years ago before the flatclams went extinct. Originally from southern coastal waters, the flatclam spread until meeting the [[Blight]] at the [[Cradle of the Visitor]], which ripped through their numbers like fire through [[sandgrass]].
- Lightweight and thin, flat white in color. 
- Once discovered washed up on secluded beaches, now only in the dark recesses of forgotten hoards and dusty shelves of collectors
### Green Lepton
Crafted in elden days in the now-lost lowland forests of [[Yr’danaar]]. Lighter than a leaf, yet carved of solid wood, the ancient [[Solothi]] traded these with one another and to those who still remembered the oaths of the [[Gilded Houses]].
- Small and light, waferlike. 
- Infused with the verdancy of leaves, retains a permanent green hue; glows with faint illumination from absorbed sunlight when left in such conditions for an hourspan
- Forestfolk and druids may pay upwards of 30 cb / coin
- Found occasionally in forests, most embedded and overgrown in tree bark; 50% chance removing them damages them beyond value
### Bronze Gudgeon
Minted in the riverine city-state of [[Al’banahr]] before the [[Timeline#War of Iron and Silver|War of Iron and Silver]] dashed the city beneath the [[Misted Sea]]. Popular among fishermen and river traders in those times, now kept as tokens of good luck and prosperity for sailors and their ilk.
- Heavier than it appears, solid, reassuring weight in the hand. 
- Sinks quickly in water to deter piracy. 
- Obverse displays a gudgeon leaping above waves, reverse with Al’banahr’s historical sigil of a skiff sailing around the sun.
- Waterfolk and sailors may pay up to 25 cb / coin
- Found in the underwater ruins of old Al’banahr, in tucked-away drawers of maritime merchants, and at the bottom of rivers and sea-shallows
### Platinum drach
Forged in elden days in the ancient fortress-cities of the carapaced [[Dwarves]]. Historically used in housel trades between noble families for their rarity and resistance to the ravages of time.
- Substantial weight. 
- Obverse depicts [[Mount Tawraba]] (TBD) with two stars above, reverse displays a mint-date in [[Proficiencies Old#High Solothish|High Solothish]]🅿. Familial house-runes edge each coin, once used for officiation of trade.
- Found in deep mines, dragon hoards, and adorning Dwarf statues
### Spirit Seed
Empty seed shells from the [[rhushaj tree]], deep in the [[Echolands]]. Only one in ten thousand fruits bear such hollowness, a rare blessing from the [[Mother Moon]]—or so druids and the [[Sisters of the Moon]] believe.
- Like a fat grain of rice in weight and appearance. In great collections, spirits are drawn, filling the empty seeds with stolen secrets—things the woods have taken from the mortal world. They glow faintly at night when inhabited by such whispers.
- Forestfolk and druids may pay upwards of 250 cb / lb (no fewer). Sisters will kill for them in any quantity.
- Found in coven stores and storage, sanctified burial sites in the wilds, catacombs and shrines to the [[Three Devout]]
### Shieldmate Denar
Stamped steel coins minted in the [[Timeline#War of Iron and Silver|War of Iron and Silver]]'s shadow, when [[Argentum Lords]] fought [[Blackhands]] and forge-smoke darkened the sky. Forged from weapon-kiln slag—hard, heavy, cold to the touch. Used in both military and civilian trade.
- Thick, solid. 
- Appears as two attached circles; tokens of the fallen and those who met the iron tide.
- Found in outpost ruins and in smaller communities once historically ravaged by the war (still traded in said communities)
### Electrum Stater
Half-in-half coins of the [[Timeline#Lycemic Age|Lycemic Age]]. Said to represent the “Arcanist Duality”, or the balance of [[Blight]] and other magicks needed to cast the crystalline spells of that age. 
- Consists of two metals: an outer circle of silver surrounding an inner disc of gold, separated by an band of the alloyed metals
- Arcanists may barter for magickal paraphernalia  
- Found in Lycemic ruins; the [[Ilanth Delvers]] use the stater as the official traded currency of their covenant
### Spun Sylmat
Silverspun spheres, delicate remnants from the [[Timeline#Age of Argentum|Age of Argentum]], were once traded among courtiers like precious gems. Turned by the hands of [[Age of Argentum#Argentum Lords|Zareth, Lord of the Silver Legion]], and his apprentices, these intricate filigree orbs began as mere byproducts of their [[Silverspinning]]🅰craft.
- Silver filigree spheres, roughly sized as five-to-a-hand. 
- Unaffected by the [[Ironmad Curse]].
- Silverfishers may pay up to 150 cb / sphere.
- Found in caches near the ancient tower sites of the [[Argentum Lords]]
# Artifacts
## Armor & Clothing
### Closed-Eye Cloak
*Cloak, Clothing*
Layer upon layer, animal skins are stitched together, one atop another. After the tenth, the mark of [[Gox the Annuller]]—the closed eye trigram—is branded onto the back; a ward against all things magickal. The wearer is invisible to all forms of magickal sense. 

**The Disenchantment**. Once per day, the wearer may touch a cursed or magickally-affected object or creature and test EGO. On a success, one magickal effect on the target is suppressed until the next dawn.

Found in [[Inheritor/Setting Guide/The Mistlands/Ashwharf|Ashwharf →]]
### Embered Carapace
*Armor as Coat of Plates*
The shed scales of a dragon, still smoldering, lashed together with blackened chainlinks into a suit of terrible beauty. The plates are warm to the touch, growing hotter in battle, and smell of char and copper. 

Found in [[Inheritor/Setting Guide/The Mistlands/Bell Watchers|Bell Watchers →]]

### Sailor’s Folly
*Magickal Stave as Shortstaff*
An elongation of human spine, vertebrae smoothed over by the pressures of the brined depths, wrapped in tattered sails and runewords. The wielder can breath underwater as though it were air and walk upon or within it as easily as dry land. 

**The Tithe.** Each day at dawn, the wielder must test EGO. Each cumulative failure binds them further to the sea:

1. Cannot take downtime turns farther than 2 hexes from a body of water.
2. Cannot naturally heal unless submerged in water.
3. Cannot breathe air.
4. Cannot walk upon land.
5. Become a Sea Witch, under the control of the Warden.

Failures can be undone. If the stave is discarded, the wielder clears one failure per day — but for that many days, they hear the calling of the sea: crashing waves, seabird cries, bubbling undercurrent. The calling ends only when the last failure is cleared.

Found in [[Inheritor/Setting Guide/The Mistlands/Ashwharf|Ashwharf →]]
# Treasure Tables
The following treasure tables can be used to stock Monster hoards, settlement vaults, or even loot held by individual creatures.

- **Table I** represents treasure found in mundane discoveries, predominantly consisting of coins and trade goods.
- **Table II** represents treasure found in ruins or places fallen within the past century, introducing the possibility of Artifacts and rarer Gear.
- **Table III** represents treasure found in dungeons or places fallen within the past millennia, nearly guaranteeing at least one Artifact and the possibility of a Spell.
- **Table IV** represents treasure found in mythological places, holy sites, or places fallen in ancient eras past such as Artifacts, Spells, and things that defy explanation.
